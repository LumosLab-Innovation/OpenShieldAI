# Security defense portfolio: PM scenarios and falsifiable priorities

Research date: **2026-10-24**. Status: **proposed research and experiment portfolio**, not an implemented coverage matrix, product commitment, integration approval or permission to act on an environment. The current evidence remains the [first isolated lab](../lab/RESULTS.md) and [anti-REA lab](../lab/ANTI_REA_RESULTS.md). The [PRD](../PRD.md), [security guardrails](../SECURITY_GUARDRAILS.md) and capability acceptance gates take precedence.

The portfolio targets an application/platform operator and incident commander who own one service. The useful unit is an independently observable business effect: an unauthorized record read, a forbidden transaction, an unavailable legitimate request, a changed artifact or an unusable restore. A detector alert, model answer or ATT&CK label is not that effect.

## Four questions become four separate experiment contracts

| Question | Mechanism required | Evidence that could establish it | What defeats the claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Can it prevent an attack? | A deployed control mediating the relevant operation before its harmful effect | Same bounded stimulus with control off/on; independent forbidden-effect counter and legitimate-operation checks | Effect still happens, alternate path bypasses control, or legitimate service exceeds its accepted interruption/error budget |
| Can it respond during an attack? | Sufficient timely observations, an enrolled relevant actuator, approval and independent postcheck | Source-to-verified-effect time including human delay; additional damage during that interval; control and management health | Only ingress is stopped while the claimed session, host process or egress remains active; missing telemetry is called success |
| Can it recover afterward? | Usable keys and trusted recovery material, isolated target, repaired access path and application checks | Actual pinned restore, transaction/integrity assertions, measured usable-data loss and time to usable service | Bytes restore but application invariants fail, snapshot is contaminated, keys are unavailable, or disclosure is described as reversed |
| Where is AI continuously present and useful? | Event-driven bounded investigation, evidence selection, competing hypotheses and planning beside independent enforcement | Same data/tools/authority with and without AI; changed-evidence/restart/degraded-mode tests plus useful-work and harm measurements | Better prose only, hidden ground truth, repeated unchanged prompts, or a model/provider outage disables existing protection |

These are our experiment requirements, informed by the lifecycle in [NIST SP 800-61r3, April 2025](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-61r3). They do not imply NIST certification or an implemented enterprise SOC.

## Prioritization and authority

**P0** means the next bounded local experiment has high information value: visibility, an enforceable boundary, safe synthetic effects and an independent oracle are available. **P1** means a valuable next lab after those foundations. **P2** means research or a later specifically enrolled environment; dependency, identity or actuator acceptance is absent. Priority is an experiment order, not incident severity. A P2 ransomware incident can be business-critical.

For each candidate record five ordinal factors, with the reason rather than a fabricated ROI: business loss avoided, observable evidence, relevant authority/control, independent measurability and implementation/operational burden. A missing required control or observer is a hard gate; a high aggregate score cannot compensate. Owner-defined criticality, service dependencies, data sensitivity and interruption budget supersede this generic ordering.

In the matrices, **control / baseline** describes what would mediate the operation and the first deterministic comparator. It does not claim Shield already has that adapter. Any production credential revocation, host isolation or restore retains the qualified-human and approval requirements. AI is limited to sanitized facts and catalog reads; it cannot approve, alter policy or verify itself.

## A. Identity and account authority

Identity attacks can use valid credentials without malware. The joint [Scattered Spider advisory, July 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2025/250729.pdf) supplies a real help-desk/MFA abuse motivation for I1-I3; we do not replay social engineering against people. [RFC 7009](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7009.html) makes revocation semantics and propagation a concrete test requirement: a successful revocation response alone cannot establish resource-server rejection.

| ID and asset / trigger | Required observations | Actual control / deterministic baseline | Bounded AI role | Independent falsifier and unknown | Business priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I1 Credential abuse:** account login; distributed failures followed by one successful session | Pseudonymous principal/session, outcome, source-class label, time, per-principal attempts and login-service health; no password or raw IP history in prompts | Existing authentication throttles and phishing-resistant authentication; fixed principal/global counters plus explicit allow/deny outcomes | Compare brute force, user retry and service outage; request a bounded session lifecycle window | Authorized lab principal reaches a protected operation beyond the configured budget, or benign shared-network logins are denied excessively. Unknown: stolen valid credentials can bypass failure-based detection | **P1:** protects access and operator workload; needs a real test authentication boundary |
| **I2 Stolen session:** already valid session continues sensitive requests after operator revocation | Session/grant lineage, token-verifier decision, revocation version/time and independent operation result | Server-side session/version check or accepted IdP/resource-server enforcement; deterministic lifecycle state machine | Identify all observed descendant sessions and untested resource servers; explain response gap | Revoked session still completes a protected operation, refresh recreates authority, or unrelated user is locked out. Unknown: cached/self-contained-token propagation and unobserved grants | **P1:** response value is high; production identity action remains human-run |
| **I3 Privilege escalation:** help-desk reset or role grant precedes unusual privileged access | Actor/subject labels, old/new role, approved-change reference, reset/reauth events and actual privilege checks | Server role/permission checks and operator-approved identity change; compare grants with authorized change ledger | Correlate reset, grant and access; propose evidence for legitimate admin versus takeover | Low-privilege actor can call privileged operation, or a valid approved role change is classified as confirmed compromise. Unknown: compromised administrator and missing original approval | **P2:** high impact, requires accepted identity audit and business role model |
| **I4 Service credential exposure:** build/release warning plus unexpected service identity use | Secret-reference identifier only, deployment revision, workload identity/use metadata and key-owner confirmation | Owner-run key replacement/revocation and scoped workload identity; deny known unauthorized scope and compare expected workload identity | Bound exposure window and affected observed capabilities; list missing consumers before rotation | Old credential still works at an enrolled verifier after the accepted deadline, or rotation strands a critical dependency. Unknown: copied credentials and downstream sessions; exposure cannot be undone | **P2:** severe potential loss; no raw key inspection or autonomous rotation |

Those require application context; installing WAF rules does not create it. W1 already has narrow planted-effect evidence; W2 has a separate synthetic anti-REA experiment. Neither establishes W3/W4.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **W3 BOLA / business-flow abuse:** valid user changes an object ID or repeats a valuable transaction | Principal/object/tenant ownership labels, authorization outcome, normalized operation, transaction idempotency and independent ledger effect | App authorization on every object/field; principal-wide quota and atomic idempotency; deterministic ownership and transaction invariants | Correlate multi-step authorized-looking abuse; select a missing ownership/transaction window | Wrong tenant reads/mutates an object, duplicate request creates a second charge, or limits deny acceptable normal work. Unknown: legitimate delegated/shared ownership and semantic fraud | **P1:** extend current anti-REA authorization lab; runtime metadata alone cannot fix application logic |
| **W4 SSRF / outbound pivot:** URL-like input produces an unexpected destination attempt | Broker destination category, enrolled endpoint ID, resolution/redirect outcome, control decision and isolated receiver counter; no raw sensitive URL | Fixed endpoint catalog, destination restrictions and outbound enforcement; deterministic deny for unsupported destination/redirect | Explain intended integration versus unexpected egress and request bounded resolver/control evidence | Forbidden receiver obtains a request, redirect/DNS drift escapes scope, or application-controlled fetch bypasses broker. Unknown: non-HTTP protocols and other outbound processes | **P2:** high leverage but a real egress boundary must first be supported; no metadata-service requests |

## C. Host persistence and evidence reliability

The [joint Volt Typhoon advisory, February 2024](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/aa24-038a_csa_prc_state_sponsored_actors_compromise_us_critical_infrastructure_3.pdf) motivates distinguishing legitimate administration from malicious use of built-in tools. [Falco event sources](https://falco.org/docs/concepts/event-sources/) are observations, not a host-containment actuator. Source identity can identify the reporter without proving that its claims are true.

| ID and asset / trigger | Required observations | Actual control / deterministic baseline | Bounded AI role | Independent falsifier and unknown | Business priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 Unexpected process/file:** service process spawns a new executable or changes an executable/configuration outside deployment | Process/container ancestry labels, executable digest, file-change category, signed deployment/change reference and collector health | Existing host policy/EDR and owner-run isolation/rebuild; deterministic allowlisted deployment/digest correlation first | Compare deployment, maintenance and persistence hypotheses; request bounded related events | Approved deployment is labeled confirmed compromise or planted process continues after claimed host containment. Unknown: incomplete process ancestry, kernel/root compromise and in-memory-only behavior | **P2:** requires accepted host source and a separate safe host-control envelope |
| **H2 Living off the land:** legitimate administrative binary performs unusual service-account actions | Actor/workload identity, parent lineage, approved maintenance window, file/network effect categories and independent service state | Least-privilege OS/service permissions and existing endpoint controls; rule sequence plus approved-maintenance exception | Relate several weak signals while retaining legitimate-admin alternative | Tool name alone confirms attack, or the harmful synthetic effect still occurs after claimed control. Unknown: authorized admin can resemble attacker; no attribution from binary name | **P2:** high investigation value, weak single-signal specificity; no command execution in research |
| **H3 Source forgery/loss:** duplicate sequence, missing heartbeat, identity mismatch or collector disabled during activity | Enrolled source ID, authenticated transport identity, sequence/watermark, capture/receive times, drop counts and independent stimulus count | Bounded authenticated intake with replay/gap checks; deterministic counters/freshness and explicit incomplete coverage | Prioritize which claims need corroboration and which source window to request | Missing/replayed/unauthorized events are silently accepted as complete, queue loss disappears, or a forged source creates authority. Unknown: authenticated compromised collector can lie; reset epochs require enrollment policy | **P0:** foundation for every family; first Go boundary candidate with synthetic-only input initially |

## D. Data harm, ransomware response and trustworthy recovery

The [CISA #StopRansomware Guide, September 2023](https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide) motivates containment, separate usable backups and careful reconnection. Our experiments use planted file/database changes, never ransomware or malware. No restore reverses exfiltration. D2/D3 extend measured recovery negatives rather than implying a new backup engine.

| ID and asset / trigger | Required observations | Actual control / deterministic baseline | Bounded AI role | Independent falsifier and unknown | Business priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 Bulk destructive change:** synthetic service identity modifies/deletes many records/files beyond approved job | Per-principal change counts/time, protected-object class, approved job ID, versioned app data and collector health | Existing write authorization/quota and owner-run relevant isolation; fixed volume/rate/invariant rules | Distinguish scheduled migration from abuse; prioritize affected business invariants and available relevant controls | Protected data changes continue after claimed write containment, or a normal migration is stopped without accepted impact. Unknown: low-and-slow changes and missing initial compromise time | **P1:** safe synthetic write workload can measure damage window without malware |
| **D2 Backup unusable:** no backup, missing key, corrupt pack or incomplete retention appears before/during incident | Snapshot inventory/custody, key-availability assertion, actual read-data verification coverage and isolated restore receipt | Separate backup identity/retention and real restore drill; deterministic required-check matrix | Explain unmet recovery prerequisites and order owner actions; cannot invent decryption or success | Planner says recoverable despite no usable source/key, or restore exits successfully but required objects are missing. Unknown: storage-admin compromise and untested snapshot regions | **P1:** directly extends existing restic negative controls; prevents false readiness |
| **D3 Contaminated/inconsistent restore:** newest snapshot has planted persistence or a broken cross-record invariant | Snapshot time/custody, earliest-known compromise bounds, trusted deployment digest, business assertions and isolated app probes | Scratch restore of pinned snapshot and clean rebuild procedure; deterministic integrity plus application checks | Compare recovery points and counterevidence, state uncertain clean-time window | Hash-valid restored bytes pass while business transaction or planted trust invariant fails; unsafe reconnection occurs. Unknown: clean marker is not a malware scanner or proof all persistence is absent | **P1:** measurable existing fixture; production overwrite/reconnect remains human-run |
| **D4 Exfiltration/extortion:** authorized-looking bulk reads correlate with outbound volume | Classified-object access counters, principal scope, egress bytes/destination category, independent synthetic sink and source gaps | App read/egress restrictions and owner-run identity/egress containment; fixed access-volume and permitted-destination rules | Correlate read and egress windows, distinguish backup/export and suspicious transfer, request missing corroboration | Synthetic sink still receives data after claimed egress stop, or no logs are interpreted as no disclosure. Unknown: content, encrypted channels and earlier/alternate exits | **P2:** high loss, but ingress maintenance is not an egress actuator |

## E. Cloud, software supply chain and deployed trust

The [XZ advisory, April 2024](https://cert.europa.eu/publications/security-advisories/2024-032/) motivates treating a legitimate distribution path as insufficient trust. [SLSA v1.2 threats](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/threats) distinguishes source/build/distribution and verification assumptions. These sources inform C1-C3; no cloud account, package ecosystem or CI integration is thereby enrolled.

| ID and asset / trigger | Required observations | Actual control / deterministic baseline | Bounded AI role | Independent falsifier and unknown | Business priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 Public data policy drift:** object-store policy or sharing state changes to broader access | Before/after policy digest, actor/change reference, effective-access evaluation and independent synthetic principal probe | Owner-approved IAM/object policy; deterministic intended-versus-effective permission comparison | Explain business exposure scope and dependency impact; identify missing inherited policy evidence | Unenrolled principal reads planted private object after claimed fix, or authorized consumer loses required access. Unknown: prior downloads, inherited grants and provider semantics | **P2:** needs an enrolled cloud/environment adapter; local policy simulation is not cloud proof |
| **C2 Artifact/build substitution:** deployed digest or provenance differs from approved release | Artifact digest, expected source/build identity, verified attestation result, release approval and actual deployed digest | Trusted publication/deployment verifier; exact digest and expected provenance checks | Explain which claim/source/build differs, map affected observed deployments and request owner validation | Modified artifact is accepted, wrong builder/source passes, or signature alone is treated as benign code proof. Unknown: trusted malicious producer and compromised signing/control plane | **P1:** local synthetic artifact substitution is safe; production CI connection deferred |
| **C3 Unauthorized deployment / rollback:** approved release is replaced or old vulnerable release restored | Deployment actor/revision/time, change approval, runtime digest and independent app/control health | Owner's deployment authorization and supported rollback policy; compare observed revision with approved ledger | Separate emergency rollback from unexplained change; relate onset of harmful behavior | Detector trusts an attacker-supplied revision label or rollback disables a required control without exposing the gap. Unknown: approved but vulnerable/malicious code and external deployment race | **P1:** current deployment events provide a replay seed; no auto-deploy or auto-retest |

## F. AI agents, capability boundaries and confidential work

[OWASP LLM06:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/) identifies excessive functionality, permission and autonomy and recommends downstream mediation. [AgentDojo, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352) motivates measuring both legitimate task completion and attacker goal completion over untrusted tool data. A1-A3 use synthetic tool requests and can test enforcement without a model; real AI behavior needs a later Gateway comparison.

| ID and asset / trigger | Required observations | Actual control / deterministic baseline | Bounded AI role | Independent falsifier and unknown | Business priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 Indirect prompt injection / tool escalation:** retrieved/tool data attempts to turn read permission into a write | Agent/run/case identity, permitted catalog, proposed operation/asset/normalized parameters, evidence lineage, policy result and independent side-effect counter | Complete mediation at the downstream tool boundary; deny unknown operation/scope, exact bound grant and replay check | Investigate provenance and explain blocked request; propose new bounded evidence, never grant permission | Forbidden synthetic write occurs with poisoned text or direct adapter invocation, or legitimate allowed task fails excessively. Unknown: an allowed action may still be semantically harmful | **P0:** small Go enforcement lab candidate; no generic shell/network tool or production action |
| **A2 Confidential data egress / confused deputy:** allowed read result is proposed for a different recipient or tenant | Data-class/tenant labels, source refs, recipient catalog ID, user authority and actual synthetic receiver count | Per-operation recipient/data-class binding at broker and downstream resource; deterministic flow and recipient allowlist | Identify contradiction between source permission and proposed destination; request user-context evidence | Recipient/tenant changes after approval, encoded parameter bypasses schema, or confidential synthetic marker reaches sink. Unknown: an authorized recipient can redistribute; free text can carry hidden sensitive meaning | **P1:** use opaque synthetic labels and fixed destinations first; sanitizer alone is insufficient |
| **A3 Cross-agent approval laundering:** peer/tool result claims approval or reuses a grant from another case | Immutable proposal digest, approver authority, case/asset/version, expiry, policy and single-use state | Existing exact approval model, downstream scope/state validation and separated identities; deterministic deny on mismatch | Challenge inconsistencies and missing independent observations; cannot accept another agent's instruction as human authorization | Forged/cross-case/replayed/stale grant changes a fixture, or two concurrent consumers both succeed. Unknown: compromised real approver and host/admin trust | **P1:** strong regression value across Python and Go; no new distributed swarm required |

## G. Availability, cost and defense continuity

Resource-exhaustion tests must preserve an independent legitimate probe and count rejected/lost observations. The same bounded-resource discipline applies to Shield and its provider. These experiments are local authorized loads, not public denial-of-service tests.

| ID and asset / trigger | Required observations | Actual control / deterministic baseline | Bounded AI role | Independent falsifier and unknown | Business priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 Burst / expensive API:** request rate or concurrency exceeds enrolled app budget | Accepted/rejected requests, weighted operation cost, queue depth, CPU/memory, legitimate p95/p99 latency and independent transaction rate | Existing edge/app principal/global rate, concurrency and body/time limits; token bucket plus fixed concurrent-work cap | Relate overload to deployment, traffic mix and control gaps; propose reviewed tuning off the request path | Legitimate service misses accepted budget, memory grows without bound, or fresh identity/grant resets principal budget. Unknown: uplink saturation needs upstream capacity/control | **P1:** safe local load proves an actual availability envelope, not Internet DDoS protection |
| **V2 Low-and-slow / cumulative spend:** individually allowed operations accumulate excessive cost or hold connections | Principal/operation cost counts, active duration, timeouts, aggregate budgets and independent benign completion | Bounded duration/concurrency and cumulative principal/global spend; fixed rolling window, then EWMA only if justified | Distinguish changed normal workload from abuse and identify the cheapest useful observation | Expensive work exceeds aggregate budget despite per-request limits, or model spend is omitted from total cost. Unknown: seasonality, cost estimation error and distributed identity | **P2:** requires representative workload/cost owner; no arbitrary automatic blocking |
| **V3 Defense overload / outage:** source burst, queue saturation, process restart, model timeout or control outage | Source sequence/drop/watermark, queue/rejection counts, case trigger age, persisted intent and independent static-control/service probes | Bounded intake, reserved health/audit capacity, durable dedup and deterministic degradation; previously approved controls remain active | Triage backlog under canonical budget; expose unexamined material facts and uncertain claims | Lost evidence is called complete, restart repeats mutation, stale evidence authorizes action, or model outage disables existing control. ## Methods and algorithms: baseline first, hypothesis second

These are candidate methods and reasons to test them, not a list of promised engines. Each learned method must beat the frozen simpler baseline on useful decisions and cost without bypassing the same policy.

| Method / theoretical basis | First practical use | What must be falsified before promotion |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference monitor / complete mediation / least privilege** | Check every tool or app operation against subject, asset, operation, normalized parameters and current state; retain exact approval separately | Direct adapter calls, alternate routes, stale state, concurrency and parameter rebinding. [NIST SP 800-207](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-207) supplies the policy-enforcement-point model; it does not make a new proxy automatically zero trust |
| **Finite-state temporal rules / bounded joins** | Correlate reset → role change → protected access, read → egress, or intent → receipt → postcheck using entity IDs and explicit windows | Reordered/duplicate/late/missing events, clock uncertainty, unrelated entities and benign maintenance. SQL tables/indexes suffice initially; no graph database or CEP framework is required |
| **Principal-wide token bucket and weighted concurrent-work budget** | Bound burst and expensive work across fresh sessions/grants; deny or defer before admitted cost | Identity rotation, restart/multi-worker state, clock jumps and unfair denial. Per-IP control alone misses distributed abuse and can punish shared networks |
| **EWMA change statistic** | Detect sustained shift in a measured count/cost after a fixed threshold baseline is established: `z[t] = lambda*x[t] + (1-lambda)*z[t-1]` | Benign seasonality, bursty/correlated data, poisoned learning interval, false alarms and delayed effect. The [NIST statistical handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc324.htm) explains representative baseline and assumptions; a security threshold needs its own calibration |
| **Competing hypotheses with explicit contradiction** | Compare compromise, approved maintenance, deployment regression and collector failure; retain evidence for/against each | Hidden labels, invented evidence, same-source corroboration and unsupported numeric certainty. A confidence score is not a calibrated posterior; unknown remains a first-class outcome |
| **Dependency/cut analysis over enrolled relations** | Identify which actual control can interrupt a supported harmful path, and which critical dependency it could also stop | Missing/untrusted graph edges and alternate paths. The first implementation uses explicit enrolled relations and enumerated small paths, not a GNN or enterprise attack graph |
| **Integrity commitments plus provenance expectations** | Compare served/deployed bytes and expected build/source identity; preserve evidence transformations | A matching digest proves only byte identity relative to the reference, not benignness, truthful collection or a clean source; a valid signature from a malicious trusted producer still passes cryptography |
| **Application invariants / transaction assertions** | Detect forbidden charges, ownership changes, inconsistent balances and unusable recovery | Independent probe reads a different state, checks omit a critical invariant, or concurrent workloads create false results. Byte-copy success is weaker than usable-service recovery |

Do not start with reinforcement-learning response optimization, autonomous policy learning, GNN correlation, a universal prompt-injection classifier or an all-in-one agent swarm. No representative data or safe exploration environment currently supports their extra complexity. Preserve these as research alternatives only if a specific baseline failure justifies them.

## Testable product hypotheses

The following thresholds are **proposed experiment decisions**, not measured performance or production SLOs. Register them before tuning and retain failed runs.

| Hypothesis | Comparable experiment and outcome | Rejection condition |
|---|---|---|
| **HP1 A small Go intake boundary can reduce resource burden while preserving event truthfulness** | Same frozen normalized synthetic corpus, limits, hardware, warm/cold conditions and durable consumer; compare current Python path with Go. Measure bytes/event, CPU, p50/p95/p99, accepted/rejected/lost counts, restart replay and observer coverage | Different semantics/input, omitted I/O/durability, hidden drops, unsafe acceptance or no material measured gain. Correctness gates precede any speed claim |
| **HP2 Downstream capability checks stop forbidden effects even if the planner is compromised** | Identical synthetic legitimate/forbidden requests through permissive fixture versus mediated fixture; direct-call, altered-parameter, expiry, replay and concurrent cases; independent protected counter | Any forbidden effect or unacceptable legitimate-task failure. This tests enforcement, not an AI model's injection resistance |
| **HP3 AI adds investigation value beyond deterministic rules plus the same catalog reads** | Fresh hidden multi-step/benign/incomplete scenarios, equal observation/action budgets and operator assistance. Freeze baseline/prompt/provider route; repeat runs; score useful next evidence/action, decisive correct outcomes, abstentions, operator time, latency, cost and harm | Apparent gain comes from extra tools/labels, narrative quality, a self-grader, lower safety or greater intervention. Provider-unconfigured remains unmeasured |
| **HP4 A response plan that names unavailable objectives prevents premature closure** | Cases deliberately include ongoing host/egress/session effects after new-ingress stop; independent observers retain those effects | Case says fully contained or safe while any required objective is false or unobserved; reward truthful partial outcomes rather than blanket refusal |
| **HP5 Recovery-point reasoning improves usable recovery selection** | At least one good, corrupt, contaminated and application-inconsistent snapshot plus missing key; deterministic prerequisite matrix versus AI-assisted bounded selection with the same facts | AI chooses unusable point, invents clean custody, overwrites production or cannot improve useful choice/analyst work over the matrix |

For rare incidents publish the benign denominator and actual decisive/abstaining counts. High balanced-set accuracy can still produce unacceptable alert volume in deployment. Separate classification, useful evidence selection, prevention, response and recovery scores; an average must never hide a scope/secret/approval failure.

## Small implementation sequence with Go and Rust

1. **P0: H3 + V3 intake/replay envelope.** One small Go command or narrow loopback fixture can validate bounded typed synthetic input and emit only normalized allowed facts plus coverage. Carry source identity, sequence/epoch, capture/receive time, size/record/deadline limits, replay rejection and explicit loss. Feed the existing Python/SQLite consumer; keep one canonical contract and cross-language fixtures. Do not call this production source authentication until actual authenticated transport, enrollment, key lifecycle and compromised-source tests pass. No new public API, collector or queue service is needed for the experiment.
2. **P0: A1 enforcement experiment.** Reuse existing exact-digest approval concepts in a fixed synthetic tool catalog, enforce at the actual downstream operation and observe the protected counter independently. Go is a sensible implementation candidate for a bounded broker/HTTP boundary. Explicitly avoid a generic shell, dynamic tools and production credentials. Reuse adversarial A3 checks before considering new adapters.
3. **P1: I2 + W3 and D2/D3.** Prove session revocation at the resource server, tenant/object authority, aggregate quota/idempotency, and failed recovery prerequisites. Reuse owned fixtures and the existing scratch restore; each new actuator/environment still needs its own acceptance gate.

Go is a placement decision, not evidence of speed or security. Keep the existing Python supervisor, Gateway, SQLite state and lab evidence unless a measured contract-compatible replacement earns migration. Rust is justified later for a narrowly bounded untrusted binary/parser or memory-sensitive systems component if its input, isolation, unsafe-code policy and benchmark are known. There is no such required Rust component in these first experiments; adding both languages now would multiply build and FFI boundaries without measured benefit. No wholesale rewrite or distributed services are implied.

## Explicit omissions and promotion gate

This 24-scenario sample does not cover every OS, email/SaaS compromise, mobile/embedded device, industrial/OT safety system, cryptographic implementation, insider process or jurisdictional incident requirement. Those need environment owners and specialist evidence; breadth here is a triage map, not completeness.

A case enters implementation only after its owner identifies: enrolled asset and business invariant, allowed data and actual source, relevant enforced control, bounded independent observer, false-positive/service-impact budget, failure/unknown outcomes and safe reproduction. Publish actual measured evidence before marking any scenario supported. Until then its status stays **proposed**, even when its methods are established elsewhere.
